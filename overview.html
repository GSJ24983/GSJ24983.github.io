My training method

Swanubhava™: the product management behavioural model

Most PM training teaches what a product manager delivers - PRDs, roadmaps, backlogs. Swanubhava trains how they handle the human side of the role: holding presence under fire, reading the room in a toxic prioritisation debate, managing a stakeholder’s anxiety.

  1. 1
    Situation triggerYou are dropped into a realistic product crisis - a live sev-1 outage, a rogue stakeholder, a crashing metric.
  2. 2
    Decision pointsNo time for a perfect framework. You decide on instinct and the behavioural cues in front of you.
  3. 3
    ConsequencesYour decision plays out. You see what it did to the room, the alignment and the product.
  4. 4
    Reinforce judgementWe walk the decision rubric together. Repeated over time, the right call becomes instinct.

The intent node

A structured choice between paths, with conflicting data. It tests whether your decision is tactically sound.

The expression node

No multiple choice to hide behind. You write what you would actually say and how you would say it, and it is graded against the behaviour’s rubric - with coaching on exactly what is missing.

Built on the Recognition-Primed Decision model, from research into how experts decide under time pressure: they recognise the pattern, then test their first instinct before they commit.

Eight behaviours, four arenas

Eight behavioural anchors inspired by the ICF Core Competencies and rebuilt for product work. Each is tested in the arenas where it matters, and every trait is something an assessor can observe in what a person says or does.

What anchor 1 sounds like

“I can’t commit to the 15th. Here are three ways we still hit the goal - which trade-off do you want to make?”

Says no to the ask, not the goal. Flags slippage early, including promises made by sales or their own leadership.

It runs on software I built

Puppeteer is my branching-scenario engine, and the method in working form.

  • Every scenario is tagged to a competency and the behaviour statement it trains. The rubric comes from that statement, so the criteria are observable, not adjectives.
  • Learners write their answer against a rubric they only see after answering, and get coaching that names what breaks when a criterion is missing.
  • Session summaries carry the competency tag, so a cohort’s weak spots show up by behaviour, not just by scenario.
  • 97 automated tests. It exports a single file that runs offline.
  • It grew out of the weekly case-solving forum I have run across three organisations.
